Sustainability

The simplest sustainable act is buying something that already exists.

Fashion has a waste problem that most brands acknowledge only in their marketing copy. The numbers are well documented: millions of tonnes of clothing end up in landfills every year. Garments worn twice and discarded. Synthetic fibres that will not break down for centuries. An industry built on the assumption that clothing is disposable.

Vintage is not a trend for us. It is the entire premise.

What we actually do

LASTGAZE is a curated vintage archive. Every piece we list was made before us, worn before us, and would have continued its journey without us. Our role is to find the items worth keeping — the ones with real construction, real fabric, genuine character — and place them with people who will value them.

We do not manufacture anything. We do not have a supply chain. We do not have a factory. We have two hands, a trained eye, and a genuine belief that the most ethical purchase is often the one that already exists.

Extending the life of a garment

A well-made piece of clothing, properly cared for, should last decades. Most do not — not because they fail, but because they are discarded before they do. The reasons are familiar: a changing trend, a minor stain, a boredom that comes from buying too much.

Every item we sell has already survived its first cycle of ownership. Our job is to make sure it survives the next one. That means honest descriptions, accurate measurements, clear photographs, and a fair account of any wear. When something has been repaired, we say so. When the fabric shows age, we document it. The pieces we carry are not described as perfect because they are not — and that is the point.

Imperfection is not a defect. On a garment with thirty years of life in it, a small fade or a worn collar is a record of use. We think that matters.

Circular fashion in practice

The idea of circular fashion sounds neat in presentations. In practice, it means something more specific: keeping clothing in use for as long as possible, across as many owners as possible, before it eventually reaches the end of its life.

We operate entirely within that circle. Nothing we sell is new. Nothing we buy is manufactured for us. We source directly — from wardrobes, from markets, from estates — and every piece we pass on stays in circulation for another round. The piece you buy from LASTGAZE may have had two or three owners before you. With the right care, it may have two or three more after.

Buying fewer, better things

The marketing logic of fast fashion depends on volume. On the idea that buying more is normal, that newness is inherently valuable, that last season's item is already inadequate.

We think the opposite. The people who shop with us are not looking for volume. They are looking for specific things: a particular cut, a specific era, a garment that will not be identical to something they already own. That kind of buying is slower. It is more considered. And it creates a different relationship with clothing — one where things are kept, cared for, and eventually passed on rather than thrown away.

We release a small number of pieces each week. That is a deliberate constraint, not a limitation we are trying to overcome. Scarcity is not a marketing strategy here. It reflects the actual reality of finding things worth curating.

Textile waste

Every year, enormous quantities of wearable clothing are destroyed or discarded. Brands overproduce. Retailers discount. Warehouses eventually empty by incineration or landfill, because the alternative — discounting below a threshold that protects the brand image — is commercially unacceptable.

We are not part of that system. We produce nothing. We overstock nothing. We have no excess inventory to quietly dispose of. If a piece we carry does not sell, it returns to the world through another channel. Nothing we handle ends in a bin.

That is not a mission statement. It is just how the model works.

Mindful consumption

We are not going to tell you to buy less. That is not our place. But we do think there is a meaningful difference between buying a piece that will be worn fifty times and buying one that will be worn twice.

The clothing we carry tends to be durable. It was made in a period before planned obsolescence became standard — before the construction of a basic garment was optimised downward to a price point. Older garments, especially the ones worth curating, were built with an assumption that they would last. Many of them have.

We ask the people who shop with us to take care of what they buy, and to pass it on when they are done with it rather than disposing of it. That is the extent of our sustainability position. It requires no certification and no third-party audit. It is just what makes sense when you are buying something that was already made.

The archive

We think of what we do as archival work as much as retail. We are finding things that would otherwise be dispersed into charity bins, household clearances, or bulk export — and placing them somewhere they will be valued.

Not every piece we find makes it to the site. We are selective because selectivity matters. A curation that includes everything is not a curation. We look for quality of construction, quality of fabric, and garments with a genuine life ahead of them.

What reaches LASTGAZE is what we genuinely believe is worth keeping.
