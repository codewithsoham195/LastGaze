# LASTGAZE — Instagram & LinkedIn

Two channels, two completely different jobs. Instagram sells the clothes. LinkedIn sells you.
Do not blur them.

---

# PART 1 — INSTAGRAM

This is the whole business. The website is where money changes hands, but Instagram is where
the drop gets built, the scarcity gets felt, and the access codes go out.

## Setup

**Handle:** `@lastgaze` if it's free. If not, `@lastgaze.archive` — not `@lastgazeofficial`,
not `@lastgaze_india`. Nothing that announces you're small or local. The brands you're
benchmarking against never signal geography in the handle.

**Profile photo:** `brand/lastgaze-mark-white.png` centred on a `#050505` square. Not the
wordmark — it turns to mush at 40px. The mark reads at any size.

**Account type:** Business, not Creator. Creator accounts lose product tagging and some
shopping surfaces. Convert before you post anything.

**Bio** — four lines, no emoji ladder, no "DM to order 📩":

```
Vintage menswear archive. New Delhi.
Eight pieces, every Saturday, 20:00 IST.
One of one — nothing restocks.
↓ Drop 004
```

Link goes to `lastgaze.com` directly. Not Linktree. A Linktree on a fashion brand reads
amateur instantly, and it costs you the click that matters.

**Highlights** — five, covers in plain white type on black, no icons:
`DROPS` · `SIZING` · `SHIPPED` · `ON YOU` · `FAQ`

`SHIPPED` is the important one — screenshots of tracking, packed parcels, delivery confirms.
It's the only thing that answers "is this account real" for a first-time buyer, and for a
brand-new store that question kills more sales than price does.

## The grid

Three-wide rhythm, repeating. Post in this order and the profile always looks composed:

```
┌─────────┬─────────┬─────────┐
│ PIECE   │ DETAIL  │ WORN    │   ← one lot, three angles
├─────────┼─────────┼─────────┤
│ WORN    │ PIECE   │ DETAIL  │
├─────────┼─────────┼─────────┤
│ DETAIL  │ WORN    │ PIECE   │
└─────────┴─────────┴─────────┘
```

- **PIECE** — flat lay, same wall, same light, every single week. This is the discipline
  that makes a grid look expensive. One wall, one time of day, no exceptions.
- **DETAIL** — the flaw. The moth hole, the repaired knee, the fade line. Shoot the damage
  as if it were the selling point, because on a one-of-one it is.
- **WORN** — on a body, moving. Doesn't need a model; you or a friend in a mirror is fine
  and often better.

Caption format, every time:

```
Lot 004 — Rugby Shirt, Ecru / Ink
1990s, UK. Size L.
Chest 56 · Length 70 · Shoulder 50

₹2,600 — link in bio
```

Measurements in the caption, not just the site. It's the single thing vintage buyers screenshot
and send to a friend, and it does more for trust than any amount of copy.

## The weekly rhythm

| Day | What goes out | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **Wed** | One detail shot, no context. "Saturday." | Feed |
| **Thu** | Reel — sourcing or steaming, hands only, no face | Reels |
| **Fri** | Countdown sticker + access code | Story |
| **Sat 18:00** | Full eight, laid out together | Feed carousel |
| **Sat 20:00** | "Open" + link sticker | Story |
| **Sat 21:00+** | Every sale marked SOLD, one at a time | Story |
| **Sun** | Packed parcels | Story → SHIPPED highlight |

That Saturday-night sold sequence is the entire engine. Eight separate Stories saying GONE
over ninety minutes teaches people that hesitation costs them the piece. You only have to run
it properly two or three weeks before people start setting alarms.

## Reels that actually work for this

Three formats, rotate them. Hands and fabric only — no talking head, no trending audio that
fights the brand.

1. **The flaw close-up.** Slow push into the damage, cut to the full piece. 7 seconds.
2. **Steam and hang.** Wrinkled → steamed → hung. Satisfying, zero production cost.
3. **The pile.** Everything you sourced this week dumped on the floor, then the eight that
   survived. Sells the curation, which is the actual product.

Post them at 20:00–22:00 IST. Cover frame must be the piece, never text.

## The part people get wrong

Do not run ads yet. Not one rupee. With eight units a week and no reviews, paid traffic will
land on an empty-looking store and you'll burn budget teaching Meta that your audience doesn't
convert. Get to roughly 1,000 followers and four clean sold-out drops organically first. Then
retargeting — site visitors and IG engagers only, ₹200/day, one image ad, the countdown as
the hook. Nothing broad.

You already know how to run Meta Ads. Don't let that be the reason you turn them on early.

# PART 2 — LINKEDIN

Straight answer: **do not make a LASTGAZE company page.** Nobody buys vintage menswear on
LinkedIn, and an empty company page with 11 followers actively makes the brand look smaller
than having no page at all.

LinkedIn's job here is different. It's where you become the 22-year-old who built and ran a
D2C brand end to end — sourcing, storefront, payments, ads, fulfilment. That's a far stronger
professional asset than the brand page would ever be, and it's the thing that gets you the
performance-marketing roles and the freelance clients.

So: your personal profile. LASTGAZE goes in it as **experience**, not as a separate identity.

**Headline:**
```
Performance marketing · Google Ads, Meta, Shopify — building LASTGAZE
```

**Experience entry:**
```
Founder — LASTGAZE (Vintage menswear, D2C)
2026 – Present · New Delhi

Built and run a weekly-drop D2C label end to end: sourcing, storefront,
Razorpay payments, fulfilment, and all acquisition.
```

Fill in real numbers as they arrive — sell-through rate, average order value, repeat rate.
Numbers you actually own beat any adjective.

**What to post** — one a week, that's plenty:

- The build itself. "I built the store in plain HTML instead of Shopify. Here's what that
  cost me and what it saved." That post writes itself and it's genuinely useful to people.
- Drop economics, openly. Units, sell-through, what didn't sell and your read on why.
- Things that broke. The failed payment, the shoot you had to redo, the pricing you got wrong.
  This outperforms wins on LinkedIn by a wide margin and it's the only content that doesn't
  read as a highlight reel.

**What not to post:** the product photos. Save those for Instagram. LinkedIn wants the
operator's view, not the catalogue.

**Banner:** `brand/lastgaze-wordmark-white.png` on `#050505`, small, left-aligned, lots of
empty space. Same restraint as the site.
