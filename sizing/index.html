Sizing Guide

Vintage sizing does not track modern S/M/L, and a tag from the 80s or 90s runs nothing like a tag today. Measurements are the only number that matters — here is how to use ours.

Why vintage sizing is different

A "Large" tag from three decades ago was cut to a different body and a different standard than a "Large" tag today — sizing has drifted, shrunk, and been re-standardised more than once since. Fabric shrinkage over years of washing changes it further. The tag on a vintage garment is a starting point at best, and sometimes it is simply wrong.

That is why every LASTGAZE listing leads with a tape-measure number, not a letter size.

How to measure yourself

Grab a soft tape measure and something in your closet that already fits the way you want the new piece to fit. Lay it flat and measure that instead of your body — it accounts for the fit you actually like, not just your raw dimensions.

Reading a LASTGAZE listing

Every product page lists exact measurements in inches alongside the era tag and condition score — no size chart guesswork required. Compare those numbers directly against a similar garment you own. If your chest measurement sits within an inch either side of the listing, it will fit close to how that reference garment does.
